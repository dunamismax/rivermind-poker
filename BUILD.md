## The 1.0 product

RiverMind Poker 1.0 is a free, self-hosted, full-featured poker website at rivermindpoker.com. It uses **play money only, forever**. People play no-limit Texas hold’em at tables of 2 to 9 seats. They can play anonymous strangers, strong AI bots, or both. The site keeps their play-chip balance, hand histories and stats, and offers single-table tournaments. People seated together can talk over live table voice, end-to-end encrypted so that not even the site can listen, which is its headline feature. It works well on desktop and phone. The bots are the best this project can train and measure, and they are always labelled as bots.

The first release after Phase 4 lets a guest practice at any balance, choose a heads-up opponent with a published evaluation, then revisit a decision from the table and play another hand. Table voice (Phase 4C) lands alongside that release. Later work gives people ways to find the game through a rules page or a friend’s link (P6.7, P6.7a). A small playtest and aggregate usage counts (P7.1a) will show where players get stuck and which paths bring them back.

The [release checklist](#10-release-checklist) at the end defines "done" precisely.

## How to work this file

1. **Orient.** Read **Now**, then [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md). Run `git status --short --branch` and `git log -5 --oneline`. Preserve unrelated work in the tree.
2. **Choose.** The owner’s current request comes first. Otherwise take **Next step**. If it is blocked, work on a ready prerequisite within the current phase or record what is needed to proceed. Phase entry conditions apply to every step under that heading, in addition to its listed dependencies. Follow the order in the file even where steps retain an older phase number.
3. **Claim.** Put the step ID, a one-line goal and today’s date under **In progress** in **Now**. Claim one step at a time. If parallel sessions share the repo, each claims a different step and stays out of the others’ files.
4. **Build and verify.** The checkboxes indented under a step are its **Done when** criteria. Verify each one through the real entry point, such as the CLI, the browser, the server or the deployed site, as well as through tests. Run the full test suites (Python and, from P1.1 on, web) before committing.
5. **Record.** Tick each criterion as it is verified, then tick the step. Add an `Evidence:` line under the step with the commit hash and the checks you ran. Keep it to one or two sentences; longer write-ups go in a doc and are linked. If you only partly finish, leave the step unchecked, tick what is verified and add a `Remaining:` line saying exactly what is left and why.
6. **Advance.** Update **Now**: next step, in progress, background runs, blockers, live site, `main` and the date. Keep each cell to the current state; history goes in evidence lines and the handoff log. When a phase’s exit criteria are all ticked, tick the phase in the [phase overview](#phase-overview) and move **Current phase** forward.
7. **Hand off.** Prepend a short entry to the [handoff log](#handoff-log). Keep the newest eight there and move older ones to [docs/build-history.md](docs/build-history.md).

**Changing the plan.** You may split a step (P3.2 becomes P3.2a and P3.2b), add a step you discover is needed, or reword criteria that turned out to be wrong. Say why in the handoff entry. Do not delete unfinished steps, lower acceptance criteria, reorder phases or change a settled decision without the owner. Raise those as an open question instead.

**Owner gates.** Obtain the owner’s explicit approval in the current conversation for each separate action of publishing, pushing to a remote, deploying to the live site or tagging a release. Decisions marked **Owner gate** and anything touching money or real identity also need approval. Server setup is already authorized under D-10. Use the stated defaults for ordinary open questions.

**Step format.** A step is one line, `- [ ] **P<phase>.<step> · Title** · Depends on: <step IDs or none>`, optionally followed by `· **Owner gate:** <action>`. Its criteria are checkboxes indented under it. After them come the record lines: `Evidence:` (the commit and the checks that passed), `CI:` (the GitHub Actions run), `Deployed:` (the revision, the release and the live checks) and, while work is partial, `Remaining:`. Phases open with a goal or entry condition and their exit criteria.

**Ticking.** Tick a box only when the work is committed, the full suites pass and the thing does what its line says, verified through its real entry point. Never delete a ticked item. If a regression undoes one, untick it and say why in the handoff entry.

**New work and ideas.** Work that a step needs becomes a criterion or a new step in the right phase, with the reason in the handoff entry. A question only the owner can settle becomes an open question with a default. Ideas that do not gate 1.0 go to [Phase 8](#phase-8--after-10-not-gating). Do not leave any of them only in a chat or a commit message.

**IDs.** Phase 0 used task IDs `RM-00` to `RM-10`, which still appear in the code docs and history. From Phase 1 on, steps are `P<phase>.<step>`. Settled decisions are `D-n` and open questions are `Q-n`.

## Phase 4B · TypeScript stack migration

**Goal.** Run the live site on the D-14 stack: SvelteKit 3, Svelte 5, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS 4, PostgreSQL 18 with Drizzle ORM, and Node through `adapter-node`, behind Caddy. **Ground rules**
- Wire compatibility. Protocol v2, the HTTP API, session cookies (HMAC-SHA256 over the same fields and secret), table snapshots and hand histories keep their formats, so the existing client, open tables, stored hands and guests’ cookies survive the cutover unchanged. Any format change gets a version bump and a reader for the old version.
- The Python engine is the reference. The TypeScript engine must produce byte-identical hand histories for the same deals and actions, and CI checks this on every push.
- Port tests along with code. A Python test that guards a behaviour gets a Vitest or Playwright counterpart before the Python code is removed. The Playwright suite is the end-to-end parity check.

## Phase 4C · Table voice

**Goal.** The people seated at a table talk to each other live, as they would at a real one, with end-to-end encryption, low delay and high-quality audio (D-15). It is the site’s headline feature. Muting yourself, muting one player and muting everyone each take one click. [docs/voice.md](docs/voice.md) holds the design: the threat model, mediasoup as the forwarding server, SFrame (RFC 9605) frames keyed from an MLS group (RFC 9420), the audio settings and what the host must provide.

**Starts now** (owner, 2026-11-20) for P4C.1 to P4C.4, which touch no table code. They add new files under `web/src/lib/voice/`, `web/src/lib/server/voice/` and `web/voice/`, and stay out of the files Phase 4B steps own. P4C.5 onward builds on the TypeScript table server (P4B.5b), and voice goes live only after the cutover (P4B.7), so none of it is built twice. Phase 5A runs alongside: the two share no files. When a session can take either a ready Phase 4C step or a ready Phase 5A step, it takes the Phase 4C step. Phase 5B starts only once both P5.9 and this phase’s exit criteria pass.

**Ground rules**
- **End to end or not at all.** No code path sends audio the voice server could decrypt. If a browser lacks `RTCRtpScriptTransform`, or the group key cannot be agreed, voice does not start and the table says why. There is no unencrypted fallback.
- **Nothing kept.** No recording, transcription or stored audio, and no stored keys. The voice service keeps room state in memory only and logs counts, durations and errors, never handles, addresses or tokens.
- **Consent first.** The microphone is requested only after the person chooses to join voice. While it is live, the table always shows it.
- **Measured, not assumed.** Delay, quality under loss, bandwidth, CPU and memory are measured and recorded with the setup used, as the engines and bots are.
- **Formats stay put.** Table protocol v2 and the HTTP contract do not change for voice (P4B wire compatibility). Voice has its own signalling protocol with a version field from its first message.

## Phase 5C · Two looks: clean and pixel

**Starts now, ahead of Phase 6** (D-18, owner, 2026-11-27). The site gets a second look, clean and professional with no pixel art, beside the pixel-art look it has today. Players switch between them at any time, the choice is kept in their browser, and clean becomes the default once it is finished. Both looks share one set of pages, components, markup and behaviour: a look is a stylesheet, a set of art and a deck, chosen by `data-look` on `<html>`. So the two cannot drift apart in what they do, and every test can run in either. Until P5C.7 the default stays `pixel`, so commits deployed for other work never show players a half-drawn look.

**How it is built.** `web/src/lib/look.svelte.ts` holds the look and keeps it in `localStorage` (`rivermind.look`). Everything that belongs to the pixel look (the kit in `pixel.css`, the wallpaper, the pixel font, pixelated rendering, card snapping, stepped animations) is scoped to `:where([data-look='pixel'])`, which adds no specificity, so the pixel look stays exactly as it was. The clean look redefines the theme's variables under `[data-look='clean']` and gives every kit class its own drawing in `clean.css`. Its art is hand-drawn SVG, written by a generator beside the pixel one and checked the same way. The cards are FeltPrep's code-drawn deck, ported onto RiverMind's 71 × 95 card box so every slot, fan and peek keeps its geometry.
