## The 1.0 product

RiverMind Poker 1.0 is a free, self-hosted, full-featured poker website at rivermindpoker.com. It uses **play money only, forever**. People play no-limit Texas hold’em at tables of 2 to 9 seats. They can play anonymous strangers, strong AI bots, or both. The site keeps their play-chip balance, hand histories and stats, and offers single-table tournaments. People seated together can talk over live table voice, end-to-end encrypted so that not even the site can listen, which is its headline feature. It works well on desktop and phone, and on the iPhone it is also a native app from the App Store whose players share every table and voice room with browser players (D-19). The bots are the best this project can train and measure, and they are always labelled as bots.

The first release after Phase 4 lets a guest practice at any balance, choose a heads-up opponent with a published evaluation, then revisit a decision from the table and play another hand. Table voice (Phase 4C) lands alongside that release. Later work gives people ways to find the game through a rules page or a friend’s link (P6.7, P6.7a). A small playtest and aggregate usage counts (P7.1a) will show where players get stuck and which paths bring them back.

The [release checklist](#10-release-checklist) at the end defines "done" precisely.

After 1.0, the owner's FeltPrep trainer joins as the site's Train section, where a player learns, studies, drills and reviews the decisions they play, and RiverMind becomes the one project and the one domain (D-20, [Phase 9](#phase-9--train-feltprep-becomes-part-of-rivermind)).

## How to keep this file

1. **Orient.** Read **Now**, then [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md). Run `git status --short --branch` and `git log -5 --oneline`. Preserve unrelated work in the tree.
2. **Choose.** The owner’s current request comes first. Otherwise take **Next step**. If it is blocked, work on a ready prerequisite within the current phase or record what is needed to proceed. Phase entry conditions apply to every step under that heading, in addition to its listed dependencies. Follow the order in the file even where steps retain an older phase number.
3. **Claim.** Put the step ID, a one-line goal and today’s date under **In progress** in **Now**. Claim one step at a time. If parallel sessions share the repo, each claims a different step and stays out of the others’ files.
4. **Build and verify.** The checkboxes indented under a step are its **Done when** criteria. Verify each one through the real entry point, such as the CLI, the browser, the server or the deployed site, as well as through tests. Run the full test suites (Python and, from P1.1 on, web) before committing.
5. **Record.** Tick each criterion as it is verified, then tick the step. Add an `Evidence:` line under the step with the commit hash and the checks you ran. Keep it to one or two sentences; longer write-ups go in a doc and are linked. If you only partly finish, leave the step unchecked, tick what is verified and add a `Remaining:` line saying exactly what is left and why.
6. **Move finished work out.** Once a ticked step's record is complete (its `CI:` and, where it has one, `Deployed:` lines), cut the whole step from here and paste it under the marker line at the top of [docs/build-history.md](docs/build-history.md), under a heading that says the date and what moved. That is an edit at the marker; do not read the rest of that file. A finished step's leftovers that are still open stay here as a line under the step or phase that will do them. When a phase closes, its section moves the same way and the phase overview keeps its one line. Durable facts, rules and lessons go in AGENTS.md or `docs/`, never only in a moved record.
7. **Advance.** Update **Now**: next step, in progress, background runs, blockers, live site, `main` and the date. Keep each cell to the current state, a few sentences at most; what happened goes in evidence lines and the handoff log. When a phase’s exit criteria are all ticked, tick the phase in the [phase overview](#phase-overview) and move **Current phase** forward.
8. **Hand off.** Prepend a short entry to the [handoff log](#handoff-log). Keep the newest three there and move older ones to [docs/build-history.md](docs/build-history.md), at its marker line.

**Changing the plan.** You may split a step (P3.2 becomes P3.2a and P3.2b), add a step you discover is needed, or reword criteria that turned out to be wrong. Say why in the handoff entry. Do not delete unfinished steps, lower acceptance criteria, reorder phases or change a settled decision without the owner. Raise those as an open question instead.

**Owner gates.** Obtain the owner’s explicit approval in the current conversation for each separate action of publishing, pushing to a remote, deploying to the live site or tagging a release. Decisions marked **Owner gate** and anything touching money or real identity also need approval. Server setup is already authorized under D-10. Use the stated defaults for ordinary open questions.

**Step format.** A step is one line, `- [ ] **P<phase>.<step> · Title** · Depends on: <step IDs or none>`, optionally followed by `· **Owner gate:** <action>`. Its criteria are checkboxes indented under it. After them come the record lines: `Evidence:` (the commit and the checks that passed), `CI:` (the GitHub Actions run), `Deployed:` (the revision, the release and the live checks) and, while work is partial, `Remaining:`. Phases open with a goal or entry condition and their exit criteria.

**Ticking.** Tick a box only when the work is committed, the full suites pass and the thing does what its line says, verified through its real entry point. Never delete a ticked item: a finished one moves to the history (rule 6). If a regression undoes one, put it back here unticked and say why in the handoff entry.

**New work and ideas.** Work that a step needs becomes a criterion or a new step in the right phase, with the reason in the handoff entry. A question only the owner can settle becomes an open question with a default. Ideas that do not gate 1.0 go to [Phase 8](#phase-8--after-10-not-gating). Do not leave any of them only in a chat or a commit message.

**IDs.** Phase 0 used task IDs `RM-00` to `RM-10`, which still appear in the code, the docs and the history. From Phase 1 on, steps are `P<phase>.<step>`. Settled decisions are `D-n` and open questions are `Q-n`.

## Phase 5D · Native iOS app

**Goal** (D-19): RiverMind Poker on the App Store as a native iPhone app that people find, install and play at once, sharing every table, invite and voice room with people in browsers. It is the next phase, ahead of the rest of Phase 6. Each step is sized for one or two sessions. P5D.1 comes first; after it, three lines of work can run side by side in separate sessions: the protocol and the table (P5D.2, P5D.3, P5D.4), the look (P5D.5) and voice (P5D.9).

**How the app is built.** These hold for every step, beside AGENTS.md:

- **One server, one game.** The app speaks protocol v2 on `/ws`, the HTTP API and voice signalling v1 on `/voice`, exactly as a page does. Nothing on the server is app-only except what the App Store needs (P5D.8, P5D.11), and a browser sees no change. The server stays authoritative (D-2): the app decides nothing about cards, turns or chips, and what it computes for display (hand names, bet sizes, replay steps) is ported from the page's code and held to it by fixtures the TypeScript writes, as the engines are held to each other.
- **Native.** Swift 6 with strict concurrency, SwiftUI and the Observation framework, `URLSession` for HTTP and WebSockets, Swift Testing for unit tests and XCUITest for the app. A web view is allowed only where P5D.9 measures that the site's own voice code is the better choice, and for content pages a player opens to read. App Review refuses apps that are a web site in a frame (guideline 4.2), and the owner asked for a native app.
- **What the app is made of.** All logic lives in a Swift package (`RiverMindKit`) that `swift test` runs on this Mac without a simulator; the app target holds the screens. Every dependency is pinned to an exact version through Swift Package Manager, with `Package.resolved` committed, and its license is recorded in `licenses/` (Q-14). No analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or attribution SDK, ever (Q-6); crash reports come from Apple's own, which the player opts into.
- **Builds in the wild.** A web page updates with every deploy; an app build stays on phones for weeks, and App Review takes days. Every server change must keep working with the oldest app build still supported (P5D.11). A change an old build would misread gets a new protocol version and a reader for the old one, as AGENTS.md already asks of protocol v2 and voice signalling.
- **Checked through the real entry point.** The iOS Simulator on this Mac (Xcode 27.2, iOS 27.2 on 2026-11-27) runs the app against a local Node server, the seeded server (`e2e/seeded-server.ts`) where a test needs a hand to go one way, and a local voice service. The Simulator's microphone gives no audio on this Mac (blockers in **Now**), so voice checks use a tone source, as the voice check page does. A real iPhone, cellular data and the App Store need the owner (D-16): those checks are gathered in P5D.16 and P5D.17, and also count toward P7.1b.
- **The look.** The app has the clean look (D-18): its tokens, its deck and its icon, drawn natively from the same sources so the two cannot drift. The pixel look's cards cannot be published until Q-8 is answered, so the pixel look comes to the app last (P5D.14) and does not gate the first App Store release.

## Phase 9 · Train: FeltPrep becomes part of RiverMind

**Starts after 1.0** (D-20): P7.7 is ticked and `v1.0.0` is released. This phase is planned and ordered, not one of Phase 8's ideas.

**Goal:** one project and one domain. The site and the iOS app get a **Train** section that does everything the owner's FeltPrep project does by then: short lessons, solved spots to study, graded drills, practice hands against labelled bots, and review that grades a finished hand and sends each mistake to a drill of that decision. Then FeltPrep is gone: nothing of it runs on ubuntu-vm, and the owner deals with its repository and domains themself. Each step is sized for one or two sessions.

**What FeltPrep is, on 2026-11-27.** The owner's own trainer, used by nobody else: a SvelteKit app on the same stack (D-14), in `~/github/feltprep`, served at `feltprep.com` as an unshared preview (`noindex`). It has a verified copy of this repository's engine, exact pre-flop equities, a CFR+ solver with its own exact best-response check, the solved jam-or-fold game at 2 to 15 big blinds, a lesson, the study view, graded drills, short-stack practice and review on a phone, and a post-flop solver under way. It keeps being built in its own repository until 1.0, and nothing here waits on it. Its BUILD.md and AGENTS.md are the record of what it has when this phase starts.

**What comes in, and what does not.**

- **Comes in:** the solver and its benchmarks, the spot contract, the solutions with their manifests and registry, grading and its plain-English explanations, the lesson reader and the lessons, the study view, the drill, review and the path from a mistake to its drill, and FeltPrep's documents on grading, the solver and the benchmarks.
- **Already here, so not imported:** the engine (FeltPrep's `state.ts`, `pots.ts`, `history.ts` and `view.ts` were byte-identical to `web/src/lib/server/engine/` on 2026-11-27), the rules document, the evaluator, the hand-history format, the clean deck (D-18) and the trained champions.
- **Left behind:** FeltPrep's look, theme, layout, name and artwork; its own practice table and its copy of the heuristic bot (Train practises at this site's practice tables, P9.5); its deploy scripts and units; its data. Nothing needs a redirect or a migration, since nobody else uses it.

**How Train is built.** These hold for every step, beside AGENTS.md:

- **One reference answer per spot.** The lesson, the study view, the drill and the review of a spot all read the same solution, so they never disagree.
- **Graded only against a qualified reference.** A decision is graded only against a solution that passed FeltPrep's grading gate: a stated game, no card bucketing, exploitability under the threshold measured by the project's own best-response code, a passing benchmark suite and a reproducible artifact. Everything else says “not graded” and why. The champions are opponents and never a reference: their cards are bucketed and their distance from equilibrium is not measured (D-7). Every claim about Train's accuracy names a row in the benchmark table.
- **Never during play with people** (D-20). No grade, study view or solver answer reaches a player during a hand at a table with another person in it. Review grades the hand once it is over. At a practice table with only bots, a decision may be graded as soon as it is made.
- **Both looks and the app.** Train is built on this site's components and tokens in both looks (D-18), dark only (Q-12), and in the iOS app (D-19, P9.6), like every Phase 6 feature. Phase 9 is done only when Train works in both.
- **Guests, not accounts.** Train's progress belongs to the RiverMind guest and is kept in PostgreSQL. A browser and the app each have their own guest (Q-15), and deleting a guest deletes its progress (P5D.11).
- **The tables come first on the host.** Solving and grading never run on the table server's event loop, and P7.3's latency budget holds with Train in use (P9.7).
- **One license.** FeltPrep's code and lesson text come in under this repository's GPL-3.0-or-later, and Q-14's App Store permission covers them (D-20). Files FeltPrep took from elsewhere keep their terms: this repository's engine is GPL already, and the evaluator's Deuces notice is MIT.
- **Imports are recorded.** Files come from a scratch export of FeltPrep at one pinned commit, and `docs/train.md` records the commit and where each piece went. FeltPrep's Git history is not merged: this repository keeps its own (AGENTS.md section 12). FeltPrep itself may be changed in any way that makes the move easier.
