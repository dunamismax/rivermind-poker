## Phase 0 completed work (RM-00 to RM-10)

- **RM-10 · Tables survive restarts.** The owner asked for this. `Table.snapshot()` and `Table.restore()` save the seats, tokens and balances, the button, `seq`, the deck generator, the log, the result and the pickled engine state. They leave out the shared evaluator and card table, so a snapshot is about 6 KB. With `serve --state_path`, the [SQLite store](../poker_ai/web/store.py) saves a table after every change and restores tables at startup. Idle tables expire after a week. Unrestorable snapshots are dropped and logged. Checkpoint tables wait while the checkpoint is missing. Tests cover these cases. A restored table replays three further hands identically to the original, and over the WebSocket a seat rejoins after a simulated restart both mid-hand and after the next hand starts. Expired, broken and evicted rows are removed from the store. Locally, `serve` was killed with SIGKILL after a player acted. A new process logged `Restored 1 saved tables`, and the seat rejoined with the same hand, `seq`, log and cards. An earlier attempt at this check was invalid, because `kill` reached the `uv` wrapper and the server process kept running. The production unit stores tables in its private state directory `/var/lib/rivermind-poker` (mode 0700).
- **RM-09 · Self-hosted at rivermindpoker.com.** The owner authorized the deployment. ubuntu-vm runs a checkout of `main` at `/home/sawyer/github/rivermind-poker` with GitHub and Codeberg push URLs. It runs on Python 3.12 from `/opt/uv-python`, and the server’s own run of the suite passed (115 tests). The RM-06 checkpoint and tables were copied to `artifacts/rm06/` there, and their SHA-256 values match those recorded above. [`deploy/update.sh`](../deploy/update.sh) installs [`rivermind-poker.service`](../deploy/rivermind-poker.service), which runs `serve` on `127.0.0.1:4316` as a sandboxed `rivermind-poker` system user (1.2 GB resident, 3 GB cap). It also installs the [Caddy site](../deploy/rivermindpoker.com.caddy), with automatic Let’s Encrypt HTTPS, a `www` and HTTP redirect, CSP and other headers, a 16 KB body limit, a rate limit on table creation and an access log registered with the host preflight. - **RM-08 · Browser client.** The owner chose FastAPI with WebSockets and a small JavaScript client. [`rivermind-poker serve`](../poker_ai/web/cli.py) runs a [FastAPI app](../poker_ai/web/app.py) under Uvicorn and serves a dependency-free [client](../poker_ai/web/static/). HTTP creates a table. One WebSocket carries the join (token inside the socket, not the URL), actions with `seq`, next hand and refresh. Every connected socket receives its own seat’s view, and errors return the current view. Table events now record chips and the amount faced, and views carry the button amounts and the final pot. [Tests](../test/functional/test_web.py) play five consecutive hands over the socket against each opponent kind. They also cover rejected stale, illegal, out-of-order and malformed messages, reconnecting, two sockets on one seat, wrong tokens and unknown tables, idle eviction and the `no-cache` header on client files. In the browser pane, against the RM-06 checkpoint and tables on desktop, hands were played with call, check, raise and bet. One reached a split-pot showdown with every hand revealed. Another ended when both bots folded; their cards stayed hidden. At 375×812 on mobile, the page had no horizontal overflow. A reload mid-hand rejoined the same seat and cards, and a second checkpoint hand ended in a won fold-out. Against random opponents, two hands were played on mobile and a third was folded on desktop. After a server restart the client returns to the lobby with a message. Browser testing found that the `hidden` attribute was overridden by CSS and that a stylesheet was served stale, and both are fixed. It also found that checks were labelled as calls and the final pot read 0, and both are fixed. Not covered: real phones and other browsers (the pane is Chromium), HTTPS and load.
- **Training schedule defaults.** `train start` now defaults to `--discount_interval 10` and `--prune_threshold 200`, keeping `--lcfr_threshold 400` and `--update_threshold 400`. This follows Pluribus’s schedule in iterations: discount every 10 minutes for the first 400, prune from minute 200. The old 400/400 pair never discounted. The RM-06 baseline already used interval 10 but pruned from 400. A [test](../test/functional/test_cli.py) pins the defaults and the 39 discount iterations.
- **RM-06 · Reproducible bot baseline.** `rivermind-poker evaluate` ([benchmark](../poker_ai/benchmark.py)) plays each seeded deal three times on identical cards, rotating the candidate through the small blind, big blind and button. Two uniform-random bots fill the other seats, and the report records every input below. Rules: three seats, 20-card deck (tens to aces), 10,000 chips, blinds 50/100, fixed raises with at most 3 per round. Tables: default `cluster` settings (50 clusters and 6 simulations per street, ranks 10 to 14), written to `artifacts/rm06/tables` in 77 minutes with a 5.2 GB peak. `card_info_lut.joblib` SHA-256 `df3c314dd851e35a3ce22d645f8d923576e8b00f040ba6ab3197904362f298b6`, `centroids.joblib` SHA-256 `036fb8f55b052cd992220600dcab0b28acd907225646570ee2e5d6775e4920ff`. Training: `train start --single_process --lut_path tables --n_players 3 --n_iterations 3000 --discount_interval 10`, with the other options at the defaults of that time (update threshold, LCFR threshold and pruning at 400; strategy and dump intervals 20). It took 42.5 minutes and peaked at 3.9 GB. The checkpoint `artifacts/rm06/baseline_2026_11_18_20_45_30_074222/agent.joblib` has timestep 3000 and SHA-256 `ba63be69b686decf6265cdbf5a215ddba2be26e8ae344efbd9b40bd29b044121`. Evaluation: `evaluate --strategy_path … --lut_path tables --deals 2000 --seed 0` over 6,000 hands gave **+134.94 chips per hand** (1.35 big blinds per hand). The standard error over per-deal means was 5.77, giving a 95% interval of 123.6 to 146.3. By seat: small blind +84.4, big blind +109.6, button +210.9. 71.1% of the checkpoint’s 15,881 decisions found a stored info set; the rest played uniformly. Two runs under different `PYTHONHASHSEED` values produced identical reports apart from `created`. The random control with the same seed and deals gave −3.89 ± 8.08 chips per hand (95%), which includes zero, and it also repeated exactly. A first pair of evaluation runs disagreed, which exposed that the deck took suit order from a Python set. Sorting the suits in the [deck](../poker_ai/poker/deck.py) fixed it, and a [test](../test/functional/test_benchmark.py) now compares dealt cards across hash seeds. It fails without the fix. Against random opponents this establishes a working, measurable pipeline, not strength against competent play. Artifacts are local and untracked.
- **RM-07 · Server-owned table core.** [Table](../poker_ai/table.py) owns the deck, turn order, validation and play-chip payouts for three seats with one to three humans. Each human holds a secret token. Every applied action increments `seq`, and requests must carry the current `seq`. [Tests](../test/functional/test_table.py) show that duplicate, stale, out-of-turn, illegal and unknown-token requests are rejected and leave both human seats’ views unchanged. Two seats inspected mid-hand and after reconnecting see only their own hole cards, along with the same public board, stacks, bets, pot and log. Only the acting seat gets legal actions. A showdown reveals every active hand and pays the best evaluator rank. A fold-out reveals nothing and hides the flop the engine deals internally. The button rotates and session balances accumulate. No network transport exists yet, so that work moved to RM-08.
- **RM-05 · Shared bot policies.** [Policies](../poker_ai/policies.py) define `SeatView`, `RandomPolicy` and `CheckpointPolicy`. The checkpoint policy restricts stored weights to legal actions and falls back to uniform for unseen or zero-weight info sets. The terminal now calls the shared interface. [Tests](../test/functional/test_policies.py) show that a view equals the view after the two opponents swap hands and that seeded decisions agree on both. They play 20 hands per policy without terminal I/O and check the fallbacks and checkpoint loading. Headless `play` runs complete and record a hand with `--agent random` and with `--agent offline` on a single-bucket table.
- **RM-04 · Consistent asynchronous checkpoints.** The server waits for every job of the dump iteration, then copies regret and strategy before sending the next iteration. One background thread writes the copies in order. With `--sync_serialise` the server waits for each write. Both files are written through a temporary file and an atomic rename, agent first. `agent.joblib` now embeds the resume state, and resume prefers it when it is newer than `server.gz`. In [tests](../test/functional/test_checkpoints.py), thread workers update a shared counter while a slow write is in progress. All six published checkpoints reload with exactly the jobs of iterations 1 to t, matching `server.gz` and the next iteration, and training continues from the last. Removing the barrier made three of those tests fail. An interrupt during either file’s write leaves a loadable checkpoint and no temporary files.
- **RM-03 · Discount schedule.** `should_discount` (`t < lcfr_threshold and t % discount_interval == 0`) and `discount` in the [CFR module](../poker_ai/ai/ai.py) replace both modes’ mixed `&` condition, which never discounted. The single-process loop also stopped indexing strategy by regret keys, and the worker stopped mutating copies returned by manager proxies, which had made its discount a no-op. [Tests](../test/functional/test_discount.py) cover iterations between intervals, at intervals and at and past the threshold. Over 14 iterations with interval 4 and threshold 12, they check that regret and strategy are multiplied by exactly the factors for 4 and 8. This holds single-process and for the server with synchronous and asynchronous discount jobs.
- **RM-02 · Resume.** `train resume` keeps the saved mode: single-process files restart `simple_search`, multiprocess files restart the server. Single-process dumps record settings, absolute paths and generator state. The agent restores update counts from `pre_flop_strategy` instead of the averaged policy, and `timestep` now advances each dump. [Tests](../test/functional/test_training_resume.py) interrupt a 4-iteration run after its dump at 2 and resume from the run directory to the dump at 4. The regret, counts and accumulated strategy match an uninterrupted run exactly. Files in the pre-RM-02 layout resume, and multiprocess runs resume in both modes. Through the CLI, a real SIGINT after the first dump of a 30-iteration run, followed by `train resume`, reached iteration 30 in both modes. Checkpoints written by the old code at `78eeb5e` resumed with `--lut_path` and reached the next dump.
